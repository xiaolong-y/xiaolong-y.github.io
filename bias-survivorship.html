Survivorship Bias ✈️

The most dangerous statistical illusion

During WWII, analysts studied returning bombers to determine where to add armor. Planes came back riddled with holes in wings and fuselage. The obvious solution? Reinforce those areas. But statistician Abraham Wald saw what others missed: the holes showed where planes could be hit and still survive. The missing planes—shot in engines and cockpits—never made it home. We only see the survivors. The failures are invisible.
