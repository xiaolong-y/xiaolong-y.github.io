Ephemeral Thoughts

Transient synthesis and observations. These thoughts dissolve after thirty days—a forcing function to revisit what matters and release what doesn't.

Some progress is backwards. Information recommendation initially seems to push civilization forward—increasing entropy for individuals and information symmetry across collectives. But it has largely increased herd behavior, political polarization, and passive information ingestion.

The best frameworks disappear into the problem they solve. When you stop noticing the tool, you've found the right one.

Via negativa: sometimes the most productive thing is deciding what not to work on.

Reading papers is not the same as understanding them. Understanding requires reconstruction—can you derive the key insight from first principles?

Writing is thinking made visible. If you can't write it clearly, you don't understand it yet.

The gap between "I understand this" and "I can explain this clearly" is where real learning happens.

Most optimization is premature. First make it work, then make it right, then—only if necessary—make it fast.

The best questions don't have answers—they have better questions nested inside them.

Simple models that are wrong but useful often beat complex models that are right but incomprehensible.

Expertise is knowing which details matter and which can be safely ignored. Beginners see everything as equally important.

The curse of knowledge: once you understand something deeply, you forget what it was like not to understand it.

Constraints breed creativity. An infinite canvas paralyzes; a limited one focuses.

The most dangerous phrase in research: "That's interesting, but..."

Thoughts older than 30 days dissolve automatically.
