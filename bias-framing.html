Framing Effect

The same information, presented differently, produces different decisions. A "90% survival rate" feels hopeful; a "10% mortality rate" feels grim—yet they're mathematically identical. Toggle between frames, make your choice in each, and discover if framing changed your mind.

The Science

Tversky & Kahneman's 1981 "Asian Disease Problem" showed that people choose differently when outcomes are framed as gains vs. losses—even with identical statistics. This is prospect theory in action: we're risk-averse with gains ("90% survive" → safe choice) but risk-seeking with losses ("10% die" → might avoid). The framing effect demonstrates that human rationality is bounded by presentation, not just facts.
